# Heinrich Zolner's invented script, Munich BSB Clm 644, ff. 51v-52r (Bischoff 1954 no. 73)

Checked 27 Oct 2026 from the MDZ PDF of the BSB microfilm (bsb00125027, 745 images; downloaded by the user in a
browser, not committed). ff. 51v-52r = PDF images 109-110 (folio "52" written on 52r).

## The book

Clm 644 is the collectanea of Heinrich Zolner alias Gurtler of Nuremberg (Latin medicine, recipes, plant glossaries
with German names, sermons), with a *Vita ac gesta doctoris Henrici Zolner qui hunc librum collegit* by Hartmann
Schedel on the opening leaves (PDF 8-13): Zolner was the son of a belt-maker (*zingulator*, hence "Gurtler"),
studied at Leipzig and took the master's degree; the book came to Schedel as a gift "in primordio doctoratus"
through his uncle Hermann Schedel (Hartmann's doctorate: Padua 1466); the year on PDF 13 reads "M cccc..." and is
cut short in the image. Schedel re-ordered the book and wrote the *Vita*.

## The script (51v: drafts; 52r: fair copy, twice)

Five base shapes, one per vowel, each named in the MS; every consonant is the shape of the vowel before it in the
alphabet, with a stroke, hook or loop added, so the alphabet falls into five sign families:

| Family (MS name) | Vowel sign | Consonants built on it |
|---|---|---|
| *punctus* | dot = a | b, c, d: dot with a tick, dot with a second dot, dot with a hooked tail |
| *linea* | upright stroke = e | f, g, h: stroke with a top hook, a side hook, a foot |
| *triangulus rectus* (point up) | Λ = i | k, l, m, n: Λ with a bar, longer leg, hook, flattened |
| *circulus* | o = o | p, q, r, s, t: o with an ascender (∂-like), σ-like, a looped ɐ/ɑ-like form, **9**, **ϙ** (o with a descender) |
| *triangulus versus* ("vel inversus", point down) | V = u | x, y, z: V with a crossing stroke, y-form, ʒ-tail |

Below on 52r: two abbreviation signs (*per*, *pro*) in the same style. 51v has three drafts of the table with false
starts (a first version gave the circle family as o ∂ σ ɐ 9 ϙ, the same as the fair copy).

Bischoff's description (no. 73) matches: "Versuche mit einer selbsterfundenen Geheimschrift, die aus den Zeichen für
die fünf Vokale punctus, linea, triangulus rectus, circulus, triangulus versus durch verschiedene Ansätze die jeweils
darauffolgenden Buchstaben (bis zum nächsten Vokal) bildet."

## Why it matters for the Voynich question

- It is a dated-context (Nuremberg, mid-15th c., before c. 1466-70), South-German, medical-book example of an
  alphabet *designed* as sign families, not a list of arbitrary signs. Voynich glyphs also fall into families
  (benches, gallows, the o/a/y/d group).
- The circle family produces **o, ∂, ɑ-like, 9** among its six members: the same shapes as EVA o, d, a, y. These are
  also ordinary Latin-script shapes (Zandbergen: Voynich letters resemble a, c, i, m, n, o and the numerals 2, 4,
  8, 9), so the overlap shows a shared shape vocabulary, not a link.
- It is still one sign per letter. Text in it would have the h2 of Latin or German (3.3+ bits), far from Voynichese
  (2.3). It shows the design idea was around; it does not reproduce the statistics.
