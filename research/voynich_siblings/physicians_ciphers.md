## What the table shows*

1. **Milieu.** Book ciphers of 1370-1470 cluster in physicians', alchemists' and recipe collectors' books: Fontana,
   Kyeser, Hartlieb, the Schedels and Zolner were all physicians. Voynich's contents (plants, baths, jars and roots,
   short recipe-like paragraphs) and its later owners (Widemann, Tepenec, Baresch, Marci) fit that milieu.
2. **What gets hidden.** Almost always *small units*: ingredient and substance names, a dangerous or embarrassing
   recipe heading, a name, a colophon. The hiding is local and the surrounding text is clear.
3. **Method.** Without exception a one-sign-per-letter system (sign alphabet, digits for vowels, Atbash, letter
   shift, foreign alphabet), often used inconsistently, sometimes with a key in the same book. Zolner's is the most
   designed (sign families); none is verbose, syllabic in output, or homophonic.
4. **Whole-book ciphers exist, and they are read.** Fontana's *Secretum* (B 67) and *Bellicorum* are whole or nearly
   whole books in a sign alphabet by a physician, c. 1420-30, North Italy: the Voynich decades and the region its
   month names point to. Both are simple substitutions and were read in the 19th-20th c.
5. **Where Voynich breaks the pattern.** (a) Nothing in it is in clear: no clear headings, no clear recipe frame,
   no key. (b) Its statistics are impossible for any method in this table applied to a European language. (c) It
   was written by several scribes in one system, where every other item is one person's habit.

So Voynich looks like a member of this genre in subject and setting, and an outlier in method and execution.
Either its makers used a far more elaborate system than anyone else in the genre (the verbose / slot-cipher
hypothesis), or the text is not a cipher of content at all (the structured-meaningless hypothesis).

## A testable prediction from the genre

The genre hides *names of things*: ingredients, plants, substances. If Voynich is an enciphered physician's book,
the short **labels** next to plants, roots and jars (pharmaceutical and herbal sections) should behave like names:
- label words should be rarer in running paragraphs than ordinary words are, and less repetitive (a drug list
  repeats a few staples, not one word everywhere);
- the same label word should recur next to drawings of the same kind (the same root or jar shape) more than chance;
- labels should not follow the paragraph text's line-initial and line-final glyph rules, which are line effects.

Those can be measured on the ZL transcription (labels are a separate locus type in IVTFF) with the scripts in
`targets/voynich/`. Some of this has been studied (label/text vocabulary overlap is discussed on voynich.ninja
and by Zandbergen); a literature check comes before any run.

## Corrections this table makes to earlier notes

- NOTES.md section 2c said Voynich's *scale* had no parallel. Fontana's *Secretum* is a whole book in cipher, by a
  physician, c. 1430. The unparalleled thing is that Voynich is whole-book **and** unread **and** statistically
  unlike any simple cipher.
